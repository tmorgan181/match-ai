# match.ai Archetypes v2 Draft

## Overview

This is the proposed public-facing 9-archetype set for the next version of the quiz.

The design goals are:

- Cover a wider range of AI positions
- Make each archetype legible to normal users
- Avoid overly academic labels
- Keep each result shareable and memorable

## The 9 Archetypes

### 1. Builder

**Tagline:** I want to build better systems.

**Short description:** You are technically oriented and solution-focused. When AI creates problems, your instinct is to improve the tools, redesign the system, or build something more responsible.

**Long description:** You see AI as something people make, shape, deploy, and fix. That means the path forward is not retreat by default. It is better design, clearer constraints, and more competent implementation. You may be a developer, engineer, moderator, operator, or simply someone who thinks in systems. You trust action more than hand-wringing, but your best version is ethically serious rather than blindly optimistic.

**Strengths:** Technical fluency · Practical problem-solving · Systems thinking

**Closest old archetypes:** Builder, Moderator

**Likely tensions:** Can underrate social and cultural harms if they are harder to instrument or measure.

### 2. Guardian

**Tagline:** I want to protect people from harm.

**Short description:** You focus first on who gets hurt. AI is not abstract to you. It lands in people’s lives, especially the lives of vulnerable people, and that moral reality shapes how you think.

**Long description:** You notice the human costs that others rationalize away: manipulation, dependency, displacement, exploitation, emotional confusion, environmental damage, and the erosion of trust. You are not necessarily anti-technology, but you think responsibility starts with care. Your contribution is grounding the conversation in real consequences rather than hype.

**Strengths:** Harm detection · Moral clarity · Human-centered judgment

**Closest old archetypes:** Guardian, parts of Advocate

**Likely tensions:** Can become overcautious if every unknown risk is treated as proof of imminent harm.

### 3. Student

**Tagline:** I want to understand AI before I decide what to do.

**Short description:** You lead with learning. You do not trust easy narratives, whether optimistic or catastrophic, and you are comfortable saying you need more evidence.

**Long description:** You may be new to AI or deeply read on it, but your disposition is the same: understand first, act second. You are drawn to explanation, nuance, and the limits of your own certainty. You want to know what these systems are, what they are not, and how much of the conversation is noise. In a polarized environment, your patience is a strength.

**Strengths:** Curiosity · Intellectual humility · Careful reasoning

**Closest old archetypes:** Researcher

**Likely tensions:** Can stay in analysis mode too long while others make decisions for you.

### 4. Optimist

**Tagline:** AI will be a net benefit if we let it grow.

**Short description:** You see AI as a powerful positive force. Risks are real, but the broader conversation feels too dominated by fear, cynicism, and worst-case thinking.

**Long description:** You are energized by possibility. AI represents creativity, abundance, scientific acceleration, better tools, and new forms of access and expression. You tend to think critics miss the upside or underestimate human adaptability. You are not necessarily reckless, but your emotional baseline is hope rather than caution.

**Strengths:** Vision · Enthusiasm · Future orientation

**Closest old archetypes:** New

**Likely tensions:** Can dismiss legitimate criticism as mere fear or resistance to change.

### 5. Pragmatist

**Tagline:** We have to keep up, whether we like it or not.

**Short description:** You think AI development is driven by competitive reality. Even if there are risks, slowing down can itself be dangerous.

**Long description:** You focus on incentives, power, and strategic pressure. Markets move, governments compete, and the technology does not wait for moral consensus. That means the real question is not whether AI should advance, but how to stay capable, resilient, and relevant while it does. You may sound pro-AI, but your worldview is often less hopeful than urgent.

**Strengths:** Strategic realism · Competitive awareness · Decisiveness

**Closest old archetypes:** New

**Likely tensions:** Can justify too much by appealing to inevitability or competition.

### 6. Skeptic

**Tagline:** I’m not convinced AI is delivering what it promises.

**Short description:** You resist hype. The benefits seem overstated, the marketing outpaces the evidence, and many grand claims collapse under scrutiny.

**Long description:** You do not need AI to be evil to doubt it. It may simply be overhyped, underwhelming, unreliable, or socially corrosive in ways enthusiasts minimize. You are open to being persuaded by evidence, but you do not think enthusiasm should substitute for proof. Your skepticism is directed as much at inflated claims as at technical risk.

**Strengths:** Critical distance · Evidence focus · Hype resistance

**Closest old archetypes:** Skeptic

**Likely tensions:** Can slide into dismissal and miss genuinely transformative changes.

### 7. Purist

**Tagline:** Some things should stay human.

**Short description:** You think AI threatens something fundamental in human life: creativity, dignity, authorship, relationship, learning, or meaning.

**Long description:** Your resistance is not only about risk or bad outcomes. It is about principle. Even if AI became safer, cheaper, or more capable, you would still worry that it flattens human value and erodes the things that matter most. You are drawn to preservation, boundaries, and human-only spaces. You are less interested in optimizing AI than in defending what should not be replaced.

**Strengths:** Philosophical clarity · Cultural preservation · Norm-setting

**Closest old archetypes:** Purist

**Likely tensions:** Can frame too many tradeoffs as absolute moral lines, leaving little room for nuance.

### 8. Antagonist

**Tagline:** AI is already hurting people, and I want it pushed back.

**Short description:** Your opposition is grounded in present-day harm: labor displacement, surveillance, manipulation, exploitation, fraud, spam, or damage you have seen up close.

**Long description:** You are not primarily concerned with abstract future scenarios. Your case against AI comes from what it is already doing now. Maybe it has threatened your work, distorted your field, harmed someone you know, flooded your community with junk, or widened the power of institutions you already distrust. You do not need a theory of consciousness or AGI to know this is going badly.

**Strengths:** Concrete realism · Present-harm awareness · Resistance to normalization

**Closest old archetypes:** New

**Likely tensions:** Can flatten all AI into one enemy category and lose distinctions that matter.

### 9. Doomer

**Tagline:** We are underestimating how dangerous this could become.

**Short description:** You are focused on existential or civilizational risk. The stakes are not merely job disruption or low-quality content. They are loss of control, irreversible catastrophe, or extinction.

**Long description:** You think the conversation is dangerously small relative to the threat. Alignment remains unsolved, incentives remain unstable, and timelines may be shorter than society can handle. Others talk about convenience, productivity, or moderation while you worry about whether humanity is building something it cannot govern. Even if your most extreme fears never materialize, you believe current complacency is reckless.

**Strengths:** Long-range risk perception · Systems awareness · Seriousness about failure modes

**Closest old archetypes:** New

**Likely tensions:** Can overweight worst-case outcomes and make ordinary governance or reform feel meaningless.

## Secondary Tendencies

The removal of `Connector`, `Advocate`, and `Moderator` as primary archetypes does not mean those tendencies disappear.

Instead, they can reappear later as:

- secondary labels
- descriptive modifiers in debrief copy
- score clusters in admin review

Recommended examples:

- "Guardian with Advocate tendencies"
- "Builder with Moderator tendencies"
- "Student with Connector tendencies"

## Tone Guidance

These archetypes should feel:

- interpretable
- non-clinical
- serious without becoming pompous
- broad enough that real people can identify with them

They should not feel:

- like diagnoses
- like political purity labels
- like niche terminally-online in-jokes

## Design Constraint

Every archetype should satisfy all three of these:

1. A user can understand it immediately.
2. A user can imagine someone they know who fits it.
3. The scoring system can plausibly separate it from adjacent archetypes.
